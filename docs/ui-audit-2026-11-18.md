# CalibrationOS screen review

18 November 2026. Written for Caleb. This is a review of the screens as they are built today (code at `e86e83f`). Nothing in the app was changed to produce it. It is not a click-through of the live site.

The product has four front doors:

1. The phone view, for the person doing the calibration.
2. The shop portal, for the glass and collision shops that book.
3. The owner app, for running the day, the shops, and the money.
4. The public website, for a shop that has not signed up yet.

Each screen below says who it is for, what it is for, what already works, what gets in the way, how many taps the main job takes from that screen, and one change: keep it, move it, fold it into another screen, or remove it. Severity is how much that change matters.

- **High.** Someone can be misled, or the daily job is harder than it needs to be.
- **Medium.** The task can be finished, with extra hunting or a second copy of the same thing.
- **Low.** Rare, or only wording and looks.

A tap count starts on the screen already open. Typing into a box is part of that step. Choosing a time, a calibration type, or a file counts as a tap.

## 1. Phone view

There is one phone screen, `/field`. Staff login sends anyone who is not the owner here. The owner can open it too, but it is not in the owner menu. It is a narrow column, meant for a phone. It shows today in Phoenix only. There is no way to look at tomorrow.

### Today’s list — `/field`

- **Who.** Caleb in the truck. A hired technician would land here and, on a job, be told only Caleb can change it.
- **Purpose.** See today’s stops in order and open one.
- **What works.** One list. Time, shop, vehicle, status. Tap a row, get the job. Empty day says “No jobs today.” Sign out is one tap.
- **In the way.** The word “Today” sits above the date as a small capital label, then the date says the same thing again. A hired tech can see the list and cannot do the work. That path is leftover from when more than one person might run a route.
- **Main task.** Open a job: **1 tap**.
- **Change.** Keep. **Low.**

### Job on the phone — still `/field`

- **Who.** Caleb, on site.
- **Purpose.** Drive, arrive, finish the job, file the Xero draft.
- **What works.** The order is right: map, call, Start Driving, I’ve Arrived, then the finish form. Start Driving and I’ve Arrived only appear on the Phoenix day of the job. Finish asks for pass or fail, the calibration type, POP or none, and a signed post-scan PDF, then “Complete job.” A free redo says it will not file a draft or charge the card. After a finish, the screen says the Xero draft number or that it needs attention, with a retry. Outside-area $30 and the $30 trip fee are on this screen too.
- **In the way.** The same drive, arrive, finish, trip-fee, and Xero actions also live on the desk board. Two screens can change one job. The address button starts with a pin emoji and the call button starts with a phone emoji. Those are the only emojis in the app, and they read like a placeholder.
- **Main task.** From a booked job today: open it (1), Start Driving (2), I’ve Arrived (3), pass or fail (4), calibration type (5), POP or none (6), pick the PDF (7), Complete (8). **8 taps**, plus whatever the phone’s file picker adds. That length is the paperwork, not extra navigation.
- **Change.** Keep this as the on-site screen. Point the desk board at it instead of repeating every button. **Medium.**

### Staff login — `/login`

- **Who.** Caleb, and any hired technician.
- **Purpose.** Email and password, then the desk board for the owner or the phone list for anyone else.
- **What works.** Short form. Errors stay on the form. A link to the public site.
- **In the way.** Under the logo it says “Field Operations · Phoenix Valley.” That is generic software language. The shop login is a different page, which is correct, and nothing here says “shop? use the shop login” in the same breath as the form. The public site footer does.
- **Main task.** Sign in: type email, type password, **1 tap**.
- **Change.** Keep. Rewrite the line under the logo in the visual pass. **Low.**

## 2. Shop portal

Shops sign in at `/portal/login`. The bar is Dashboard, My Jobs, Book, Scorecard, Settings, then the shop name and Sign Out. Five links sit in one row. There is no Menu button. On a phone that row crowds. Shops book from phones. That is a **high** layout problem for this whole area.

### Shop login — `/portal/login`

- **Who.** A shop contact.
- **Purpose.** Sign in. Old public booking and scorecard links land here with a note that the shop should log in.
- **What works.** Email, password, a clear error, forgot-password link, and a link to create an account. The note from an old link is shown.
- **In the way.** It is a second login that looks almost the same as staff login, with “Shop Portal” on the logo. That difference is worth keeping so a shop never opens the desk board.
- **Main task.** Sign in: **1 tap** after the two fields.
- **Change.** Keep. **Low.**

### Forgot password — `/portal/forgot`

- **Who.** A shop contact who cannot sign in.
- **Purpose.** Email a one-time reset link.
- **What works.** One field, one button, a plain result, a way back.
- **In the way.** Nothing important.
- **Main task.** **1 tap.**
- **Change.** Keep. **Low.**

### Choose a new password — `/portal/reset/:token`

- **Who.** The person who opened the email link.
- **Purpose.** Set a new password.
- **What works.** One field. Too-short passwords are stopped. A dead link says so. Done offers Sign in.
- **In the way.** Nothing important.
- **Main task.** Type the password, **1 tap**.
- **Change.** Keep. **Low.**

### Accept an invite — `/portal/invite/:token`

- **Who.** A shop Caleb invited, before they have a password.
- **Purpose.** Name, password, sign the agreement, accept the charge terms, save a card.
- **What works.** A bad or used link says it will not open. The agreement is on the page. The charge-terms checkbox matches the public signup. No charge today is said in plain words. After the card, they can book.
- **In the way.** It is a long form, and it repeats the public signup. Both are needed: one is for a shop that finds the website, one is for a shop Caleb adds. They should ask for the same things in the same order, which they now nearly do.
- **Main task.** Fill the form, check the agreement, check the charge terms, continue, save the card. About **4 taps** plus typing. A second sitting appears if the card is saved later.
- **Change.** Keep. **Low.**

### You’re all set — `/portal/ready`

- **Who.** A shop that just finished signup or an invite.
- **Purpose.** Confirm the agreement and card, then book or enter the portal.
- **What works.** Two clear buttons. Says there is no public booking link.
- **In the way.** Signup itself goes straight to booking, so this screen is easy to miss. It is a nice confirmation and not a required step.
- **Main task.** Book: **1 tap**.
- **Change.** Keep as the end of invite setup. **Low.**

### Card setup return — `/portal/setup-return`

- **Who.** A shop coming back from Stripe after saving a card.
- **Purpose.** Tell them the card saved, the setup was cancelled, or it failed, and collect charge terms if still needed.
- **What works.** Separate messages for saved, cancelled, missing, and failed. A path onward.
- **In the way.** People only see it when Stripe sends them back. It should stay quiet and specific, which it is.
- **Main task.** Read, then **1 tap** to continue.
- **Change.** Keep. **Low.**

### Dashboard (day map) — `/portal`

- **Who.** A shop, during the day.
- **Purpose.** See where their job sits among the day’s stops, without showing other shops’ details. Other stops show a time only.
- **What works.** The date, a map, their own jobs with a link to the job, and a full-width “Book a new calibration.” If booking is closed, a banner says why and sends them to update the card or accept the charge terms. Empty day explains that other stops stay private.
- **In the way.** “Day map” is a small capital label above a date that already says the day. Their jobs on this page, My Jobs, and Scorecard are three lists of the same cars. The map is the part that exists only here, and it is the reason to keep the page.
- **Main task.** See today’s stop: **0 taps** if it is today. Change day: **1 tap** on the date. Open a job: **1 tap**. Book: **1 tap**.
- **Change.** Keep the map. Drop the repeated job essays once Scorecard is folded in. **Medium.**

### My Jobs — `/portal/jobs`

- **Who.** A shop, looking up a car.
- **Purpose.** Every job for that shop: all, upcoming, completed, cancelled.
- **What works.** Filters are one tap. Each row is time, date, vehicle, service, price, status. Upcoming still counts an old status name, `dispatched_to_contractor`. No current screen sets that status. It is a leftover word, and it does not add a row by itself.
- **In the way.** The dashboard already lists today’s jobs. This page earns its place as the history.
- **Main task.** Open a job: **1 tap**, or **2** if a filter is needed.
- **Change.** Keep. **Low.**

### One job — `/portal/jobs/:id`

- **Who.** A shop, checking or changing a booking.
- **Purpose.** Vehicle, time, price, paperwork, edit, cancel, download a report.
- **What works.** Status is plain. A held time says the owner still has to approve it. Edit and Cancel sit together and disappear after the job is done, cancelled, or declined. Download errors stay on the page.
- **In the way.** Cancel uses the browser’s default confirm box, and the confirm text says the shop contact gets an email. While shop email is off, that email is held for Caleb to read and is not sent. The sentence overpromises.
- **Main task.** Cancel: **2 taps** (button, then the browser confirm). Download: **1 tap**.
- **Change.** Keep. Fix the cancel sentence so it matches whether mail is actually sending. **Medium.**

### Book — `/portal/book`

- **Who.** A shop with a card and accepted charge terms.
- **Purpose.** Book a calibration in two steps.
- **What works.** Step 1 is place, date, and time, with the shop address already filled in. Times that need Caleb’s OK are marked “Pending approval.” Step 2 is the vehicle, the calibration and price, a warranty redo, “invoice instead,” phone, and notes. Closed booking says paused or closed and sends them to the card or the charge terms. Success says confirmed or pending, and offers My Jobs.
- **In the way.** The owner books the same job on a different, longer form (five steps). The two forms can drift. This shorter one is the one to keep as the model.
- **Main task.** Pick a time, Next, fill the vehicle, choose the service, confirm. About **4 taps** plus typing, when the address stays the shop.
- **Change.** Keep. Later, make the owner form a close cousin of this one. **Medium**, because of the twin form, not because this page is wrong.

### Scorecard — `/portal/scorecard`

- **Who.** A shop, looking at the last 30 days.
- **Purpose.** Four numbers: jobs, completed, upcoming, dollars. Then upcoming cars and recent completions, and another Book button.
- **What works.** The four numbers are the only new information. The address and East or West badge repeat the account.
- **In the way.** Upcoming and recent cars are already on My Jobs and, for today, on the dashboard. A third Book button repeats the bar and the dashboard.
- **Main task.** Read the month: **0 taps**.
- **Change.** Move the four numbers onto the dashboard or the top of My Jobs. Remove this tab. **Medium.**

### Settings — `/portal/settings`

- **Who.** A shop contact.
- **Purpose.** See the account, the card on file, replace the card, accept charge terms, read the agreement.
- **What works.** Name, email, shop, and address are readable. The card shows brand and last four. “I accept the charge terms” appears when a signed shop has a good card and has not accepted yet. The agreement is on the page.
- **In the way.** Account, card, terms, and agreement are four stacked blocks of the same gray panel. Fine for a settings page. The charge-terms block is the one that must stay obvious, because booking stays closed until it is done.
- **Main task.** Accept terms: **1 tap**. Replace a card: start, then the card form.
- **Change.** Keep. **Low.**

## 3. Owner app

The owner bar, from `frontend/src/components/Shell.jsx`, is:

Dispatch, New shops, Days off, Email preview, Shops, Invites, Charges, Invoices, Messages, Growth, Settings.

That is 11 links when Growth is on. The example build turns Growth on. A Menu button collapses them on a small screen. A chat bubble (Assist) can sit on every owner page when a Google key is set. It is hidden when the key is missing. It can answer questions and will not change a job until Caleb confirms. It is not a tab. Keep it that way.

These owner screens are not in the bar: add a shop, a shop’s page, book for a shop, addresses to check, the phone view, and the email approve/decline link.

### Dispatch — `/dashboard`

- **Who.** Caleb, at a desk or with the laptop.
- **Purpose.** Run one day: who is where, what is next, what got cancelled.
- **What works.** Prev, Today, Next. Jobs, revenue, estimated miles, and done-by follow the jobs still on the board, so deleting the last job clears the miles and the finish time. Miles are drive minutes times one half (about 30 mph), not a mapped route. The map, a tomorrow strip, drag to reorder, and refresh locations are all here. A job card can approve or decline a held time, start driving, arrive, finish, reschedule, cancel, edit, delete, mark outside area, charge a $30 trip fee, switch a job to invoice, charge the card, and retry a Xero draft. Cancelled and declined open on a separate list. New bookings since last look are an orange pill. While shop mail is held, a yellow link reads **“Shop emails: OFF (testing)”** and opens Email preview.
- **In the way.** The card is the busy part. A single job shows a crowd of buttons that matter at different times. The phone view repeats the driving and finishing ones. The yellow link is developer language on the screen used all day. The new-bookings pill stores the time from the moment of booking. Moving the job updates the card and does not update the pill, so the pill can still show the original time. Cancelling the job does clear that pill. Tomorrow’s strip still counts “West” and “East” while Settings says booking no longer splits the valley.
- **Main task.** Move a booked job forward: Start Driving is **1 tap** on the card. Mark the new bookings seen: open the pill, then Mark all seen, **2 taps**. Finish from this card is the same long form as the phone.
- **Change.** Keep this as the home screen. Show the next action on the card and put charge, delete, trip fee, and Xero inside the job. Point “do this in the truck” at the phone view. **High**, because this is the all-day screen and two of its notices are wrong or cryptic (the moved-job time, and the email chip).

### New shops — `/requests`

- **Who.** Caleb, when someone signs up on the website.
- **Purpose.** See self-serve shops. Flag one. Suspend one so they cannot book. A second table, “Earlier requests,” approves or declines signups from before shops could create themselves.
- **What works.** The first table matches how signup works now: they are already shops, and Caleb does not approve them first. Flag and Suspend are clear. An old approval still creates the shop and an invite, and if mail is off it shows the setup link as held.
- **In the way.** The page title and the first paragraph are about shops that already exist. The second table is a different, older task. Invites has its own page. A brand-new website signup also does not appear as a row Caleb must work, unless he wants to flag or suspend it.
- **Main task.** Suspend: **1 tap**. Approve an old request: **1 tap**.
- **Change.** Move the live table onto Shops as a “New” filter. Keep the old table only while old rows are still pending, then remove it. **Medium.**

### Days off — `/days-off`

- **Who.** Caleb, a few times a year.
- **Purpose.** Close a holiday or a vacation range so shops cannot book it. Jobs already on that day stay, and are listed so he can move them.
- **What works.** The explanation is honest: nothing is cancelled for him. Holidays can be opened or closed. A custom range can be added or removed. A link at the bottom goes to addresses whose pin was a guess.
- **In the way.** It is a top-level tab for an occasional task. The real “when can people book” control is here, while Settings → Schedule still shows East and West clocks and says those clocks are not used to place jobs.
- **Main task.** Close a range: pick start, pick end, **1 tap** to add.
- **Change.** Move onto Settings, in place of the unused schedule clocks. **Medium.**

### Email preview — `/email-preview`

- **Who.** Caleb, while shop and customer mail is held for testing.
- **Purpose.** Read messages that were not sent. The page says nothing here sends mail.
- **What works.** Subject, who it was for, Phoenix time, Read or Hide. Empty state is plain.
- **In the way.** It is a top-level tab for a temporary mailbox. The desk board also links here, under the words “Shop emails: OFF (testing).” Two doors, and the one on the board sounds like a broken feature.
- **Main task.** Read one message: **1 tap**.
- **Change.** Move under Settings. On the board, say “Shop emails are held” and link to that settings spot. Remove it from the bar when mail is actually sending. **High** for the wording on the board, **medium** for the tab itself.

### Shops — `/shops`

- **Who.** Caleb, looking up a customer.
- **Purpose.** Search, add, edit, or book for a shop.
- **What works.** Search, name and address, zone, status, Edit, Book, Add Shop. A failed load says to reload.
- **In the way.** The **Tier** column prints `pricing_tier`. That field is Standard or Custom. Website signup always stores Standard. The rates that actually price a job and a Xero draft come from the wholesale card on the shop (Standard, Real Fast, Preferred, custom, or “match the name”). A Real Fast or Preferred shop can still say Standard in this column. The column and the card are different facts.
- **Main task.** Open a shop: **1 tap**. Book: **1 tap**.
- **Change.** Keep the list. Replace Tier with the wholesale card name people are actually billed on. **High.**

### Add a shop — `/shops/new`

- **Who.** Caleb, adding a shop that did not use the website.
- **Purpose.** Name, mobile, email, address, standard or custom rates, then send an invite.
- **What works.** Address has to be confirmed. A duplicate name or email can be saved on purpose. After save, Send invite explains held versus sent, and there is no public booking link.
- **In the way.** “Pricing tier” here is Standard or Custom rates. The shop page later calls the same idea a wholesale card, with more choices (Real Fast, Preferred, match the name). Two names for price. Invites can also be sent from the Invites tab, so the follow-up exists twice.
- **Main task.** Save, then send the invite: **2 taps** plus the form.
- **Change.** Keep. Use the same rate names as the shop page. **Medium.**

### One shop — `/shops/:id`

- **Who.** Caleb, checking a customer.
- **Purpose.** Edit name, status, contacts, address, Xero contact id, and wholesale card. Book a job for them.
- **What works.** Status includes active, paused, inactive, suspended. The wholesale card is a plain dropdown. The Xero note says a contact is never created. Address changes update the pin and do not email the shop.
- **In the way.** The page does not list this shop’s jobs. It does not show whether a card is on file, the brand, the last four, or a failed card. Those facts exist on the shop record and are what decide whether the shop can book. Notes are saved if they were already there, and there is no notes box on the page. Save uses the browser’s alert box that just says “Saved.”
- **Main task.** Change the card and save: **2 taps**. Find the shop’s jobs: not possible here. He has to leave and scan Dispatch or book again.
- **Change.** Keep, and add a jobs list plus card status on this page. **High.**

### Book for a shop — `/shops/:id/book`

- **Who.** Caleb, booking on a shop’s behalf.
- **Purpose.** A five-step form: where, when, vehicle, calibration and price, review.
- **What works.** He can type a time, not only pick a slot. Price buttons show the shop’s rate. Pending slots are labeled.
- **In the way.** The shop’s own form is two steps and includes warranty. This one is five steps and splits vehicle and calibration. The review step can say “Please invoice me instead” and “until the owner approves it,” which is shop language on Caleb’s screen. He is the owner.
- **Main task.** Continue through five steps, then confirm: **5 taps** plus typing and a time choice.
- **Change.** Fold the extra steps into one form shared with the shop, and keep “set a time” for Caleb only. **High.**

### Invites — `/invites`

- **Who.** Caleb, sending a portal link.
- **Purpose.** Pick a shop, send an invite, copy a link, resend, see held versus sent.
- **What works.** Held mail is labeled. The link can be copied. A manual-send queue appears when mail cannot go out.
- **In the way.** Add a shop already sends an invite. New shops can also surface a setup link. This is a third door for the same letter.
- **Main task.** Send: pick shop (the email fills in), **1 tap**.
- **Change.** Move onto the shop page as “Send portal invite.” **Medium.**

### Charges — `/charges`

- **Who.** Caleb, after a job that should hit the card.
- **Purpose.** List completed jobs waiting for a card charge. Charge now, or skip and bill outside Stripe.
- **What works.** Shop, vehicle, completed time, amount, card brand and last four, and the error if a charge failed. Empty state is plain.
- **In the way.** Dispatch can already “Charge card now” on the job. Two places to take the same money. Skip asks with the browser confirm box.
- **Main task.** Charge one job: **1 tap**.
- **Change.** Keep the list, and put it with Invoices on one Money screen. Leave a single charge action on the job. **Medium.**

### Invoices — `/invoices`

- **Who.** Caleb, and Maddy when she wants the app’s copy of what went to Xero.
- **Purpose.** One row per completed job: id, shop, status, total, due, created. A Xero link when there is one. Late fees if any were applied.
- **What works.** The intro says the Xero record stays a draft until someone sends it in Xero. That matches how Maddy works. Paid and open are readable.
- **In the way.** It is a mirror. The work of sending the invoice happens in Xero, not here. As its own tab it looks equal to Dispatch.
- **Main task.** Open a Xero invoice: **1 tap**.
- **Change.** Keep the list, under Money, beside Charges. **Medium.**

### Messages — `/messages`

- **Who.** Caleb, if he opens it.
- **Purpose.** A log of texts.
- **What works.** Each row shows the number, the time, the kind of message, and the body. Empty says “No messages yet.”
- **In the way.** The page says “Mock SMS log — these would send via Twilio in production.” Texts are on hold. This is a developer note in the main menu. The public site still tells shops they get job updates by text.
- **Main task.** Read a text: **0 taps** once the page is open. There is nothing to send.
- **Change.** Remove it from the bar. Keep the log under Settings until texts are real, and do not describe it as production. **High** for the public promise, **medium** for the tab.

### Growth — `/growth`

- **Who.** Caleb, for relationship follow-up, not for today’s route.
- **Purpose.** A contact list in circles (Top 5, Key 50, Vital 100, Prospect), today’s nudges, and introductions. Approve, then send.
- **What works.** When the page is on, the circles and the nudge buttons are understandable. Settings shows a contact count and a link here.
- **In the way.** The menu shows Growth unless the build flag is the word false. The page itself only shows the lists when the flag is the word true. The example build sets true, so a normal build shows the lists. Any other value opens a splash that says “PARTNER GROWTH ENGINE,” “Module 9 – feature-flagged OFF,” and “see docs/module9/README.md.” That splash is internal. The live page opens with “Power Connector 5+50+100,” which is a method name, not a task.
- **Main task.** Approve a nudge: **1 tap**. Send it: a second tap, on purpose.
- **Change.** Move out of the daily bar into Settings. Rewrite the title as a plain task, and do not show the internal splash to Caleb. **Medium.**

### Settings — `/settings`

- **Who.** Caleb, when something about the business changes.
- **Purpose.** Four inner tabs: Pricing, Technicians, Schedule, Integrations.
- **What works.** Standard prices are edited and saved. Real Fast and Preferred rates are a second card on the same pricing tab, and the note says Xero drafts use those amounts. Technicians can be edited and added. The owner row is marked Owner. Integrations can connect or disconnect Xero, and it shows Maps, Assist, Growth, texting, and calendar in plain status lines.
- **In the way.** Schedule says the East and West clocks are leftover and are not used to place jobs, then still offers Start, End, Max jobs, Slot, and Buffer with Save. Saving looks like it changes the day. It does not. Integrations is full of setup language (“Mock mode,” “Deferred to next iteration,” “set GOOGLE_API_KEY”). Useful while going live, noisy after.
- **Main task.** Change a standard price and save: **2 taps**. Connect Xero: **1 tap**, then Xero’s own site.
- **Change.** Keep. Replace Schedule with Days off. Hide integration rows that are only instructions once the service is live. **Medium.**

### Addresses to check — `/map-pins`

- **Who.** Caleb, after a pin was saved as a guess.
- **Purpose.** Re-check shop and job addresses.
- **What works.** Shops and jobs are split. Recheck is one button each. Empty state is clear. The only door is a link on Days off.
- **In the way.** Easy to forget, which is acceptable if guesses are rare. It should not become a tab.
- **Main task.** Recheck one address: **1 tap**.
- **Change.** Keep. Leave it linked from Days off (or from Settings, once Days off moves). **Low.**

### Approve or decline from email — `/owner-action/:token`

- **Who.** Caleb, from a mail link, without signing in.
- **Purpose.** Confirm before a held booking is approved or declined.
- **What works.** The job is summarized. One confirm button. A bad link says so. It is not in the menu, which is correct.
- **In the way.** Nothing important. Dispatch can approve the same job.
- **Main task.** **1 tap.**
- **Change.** Keep. **Low.**

### Assist — bubble on owner pages

- **Who.** Caleb, if the Google key is set.
- **Purpose.** Ask about the day. Changes wait for a confirmation card.
- **What works.** It stays off the menu. It is absent when not configured.
- **In the way.** It is a second way to reschedule, cancel, and reorder, beside the board. That is fine while every change still asks.
- **Main task.** Open, type, send: **2 taps** plus the question. A change is an extra confirm.
- **Change.** Keep. **Low.**

## 4. Public website

The public header, from `frontend/src/marketing/SiteChrome.jsx`, is Home, Services, Pricing, Service Area, About, FAQ, Contact, then Staff login (or Dispatch if already signed in) and **Book a job**. The footer repeats those pages plus Create a shop account and Shop login.

“Book a job” opens signup when nobody is signed in to the portal, and opens the portal book form when they are. A new shop cannot book before an account, a card, and the agreement. The button name skips that.

Home already contains services, prices, the first-job offer, a short about, reviews, and the FAQ. Several header links open a second copy.

### Home — `/`

- **Who.** A shop owner deciding whether to call.
- **Purpose.** The pitch, the prices, how signup works, and the phone number’s world (Gilbert, Monday–Saturday, English and Spanish).
- **What works.** Real prices, real first-job offer, real reviews, real phone and email. The steps match the product: account, then book, then the visit.
- **In the way.** Every section starts with a small orange label (“How it works,” “Services and pricing,” and so on) above a headline that repeats it. A fake app picture shows a sidebar of Bookings, Reports, and Account. The real portal sidebar is Dashboard, My Jobs, Book, Scorecard, Settings. One benefit says “Real-time ETAs and job updates by text.” Texts are not sending. The FAQ block is also its own page.
- **Main task.** Create an account: **1 tap**. Call: **1 tap** on the phone number in the footer.
- **Change.** Keep as the only pitch page. Fix the texting sentence and the fake app. **High** for the texting claim.

### Services — `/services`

- **Who.** A shop comparing the work.
- **Purpose.** Static, dynamic, dual, triple, Tesla, glass shops versus collision shops.
- **What works.** The split between glass and collision is clearer here than on Home.
- **In the way.** Prices and “job updates by text” repeat Home and Pricing.
- **Main task.** Reach signup: **1 tap**.
- **Change.** Keep the collision-versus-glass copy, and move it onto Home or Pricing. Drop the header link. Leave the address working so old links do not break. **Medium.**

### Pricing — `/pricing`

- **Who.** A shop comparing dollars.
- **Purpose.** The five rates, the $50 first job, the 5-star guarantee, same-day add-ons, the referral.
- **What works.** This is the page a shop will send to a manager. The numbers match Home.
- **In the way.** Home already shows the same five prices and the same guarantee. The referral is the piece Home does not spell out as clearly.

## 6. How it looks

This section is for a later polish pass. The goal of that pass is a distinctive, custom feel. These are the spots that still look like a template, a component kit, or an internal demo.

What already has a point of view, and should stay:

- Gunsteel backgrounds (`#2A2E37`, `#343843`) and brand orange (`#FF5F1F`) with near-black type on the orange. Defined in `frontend/src/index.css` and again in `frontend/src/marketing/marketing.css`.
- The stencil wordmark (Saira Stencil One) and the orange “OS” block. DM Sans for reading, JetBrains Mono for times and money.
- The real logo on the public site, the real map, the real reviews, Gilbert, and the phone number.
- Plain operational sentences on booking and days off. Those sound like a person.

What looks generic or unfinished:

- **The same admin card, over and over.** Shops, Invites, Invoices, New shops, Days off, Email preview, Messages, and Charges are one pattern: a large title, a gray paragraph, a gray panel, an all-caps tiny table header, and ghost buttons. The table header style is copied in `ShopsList.jsx`, `Invites.jsx`, `Invoices.jsx`, and `Requests.jsx`. Nothing about a shop list looks different from an invoice list. A polish pass can keep one table style and still give Today, a shop, and Money their own layout.
- **Default status colors.** Blue `#3B82F6`, green `#10B981`, red `#EF4444`, and amber `#F59E0B` in `index.css` are the stock Tailwind palette. A purple `#8B5CF6` is declared and never used. The badges feel like a UI kit sitting on top of an otherwise custom background.
- **A component library that the screens do not use.** `frontend/src/components/ui/` has a large set of stock widgets (accordion, dialog, carousel, and the rest). The real screens use hand-made `.btn`, `.surface`, and `.input` plus inline styles. The unused kit is not what people see. It is a sign the app was started from a template and then skinned.
- **Small-capital labels above titles.** “Today” on the phone (`Field.jsx`), “Day map” on the shop dashboard (`PortalDashboard.jsx`), “Field Operations · Phoenix Valley” on staff login (`Login.jsx`), and a pill or kicker on every public section (`.eyebrow` and `.section-kicker` in `marketing.css`, used all through `HomePage.jsx`, `InfoPages.jsx`, and `ContactPage.jsx`). The headline underneath usually repeats the label.
- **A fake product screenshot.** `HomePage.jsx` draws a window with traffic-light dots and a sidebar: Bookings, Reports, Account. That is not the portal. It is a marketing mock wearing the product’s colors.
- **Two display fonts.** The public site uses Oswald for headlines. The app uses Saira Stencil. Both are loud. Together they feel like two brands.
- **Emoji as icons.** `Field.jsx` puts a pin before the address and a phone before “Call shop.”
- **Developer sentences on owner screens.** “Shop emails: OFF (testing)” on Dispatch. “Mock SMS log — these would send via Twilio in production.” on Messages. “Module 9 – feature-flagged OFF” and “Power Connector 5+50+100” on Growth. Settings integrations: “Mock mode,” “Deferred to next iteration,” “set GOOGLE_API_KEY,” “set GROWTH_MODULE_ENABLED=true.” Schedule’s “these clocks are leftover.”
- **Browser default popups.** Shop save says “Saved” in a system alert (`ShopDetail.jsx`). Charge skip, Xero disconnect, and shop cancel use the browser’s confirm box.
- **Numbered marketing steps and quote cards.** Home’s 01 / 02 / 03 and the review grid are a familiar landing-page pattern. The words inside are specific. The frame is not.
- **Public claims that the product does not do yet.** “Real-time ETAs and job updates by text” on Home, and “Job updates by text” on Services. About’s “online scheduler.” Those lines make the site sound like a finished SaaS brochure.

Polish order, when that pass happens: rewrite the developer sentences and the texting claim first, then the fake app and the kickers, then give Today, a shop, and Money layouts that are not the same table. Leave the gunsteel, the orange, and the stencil wordmark alone.

## 7. Suggested order of work

Each step is one reviewable change. Earlier steps remove wrong information. Later steps move navigation, after each screen has a place to land.

1. **Correct the facts.** Shops list shows the wholesale card, not Tier. The shop page lists that shop’s jobs and the card on file. The new-bookings pill uses the job’s current time. The board says shop mail is held, in normal words.
